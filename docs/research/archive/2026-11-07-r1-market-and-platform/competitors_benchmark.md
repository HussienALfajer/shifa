# Competitive Benchmark: Clinic Practice-Management SaaS and Doctor-Booking Platforms (Global, MENA, Turkey, Syria) — as of Nov 2026

Scope note: web research only, done in November 2026. Pricing on aggregator sites (Capterra, CostBench, FitGap, etc.) is often stale. Where a vendor's own page was read, that is stated. Older data is marked with its year.

---

## 1. Global practice-management + booking leaders: segment, features, pricing, patient side, multi-location, extensibility

### Takeaway
The global leaders split into three models:
- **Marketplace + SaaS bundles sold as a monthly subscription:** Doctolib, Docplanner/Doctoralia, Practo.
- **Pure pay-per-new-patient marketplace:** Zocdoc.
- **Pure practice-management SaaS, priced per practitioner or in practitioner bands:** Jane, Cliniko, SimplePractice, Halaxy, dental PMS.

Typical list prices for a solo doctor are about US$45–150/month in Western markets and about ₹1,500–2,000/month (~US$18–24) in India. Multi-location, unlimited admin users and SMS reminders are now table stakes. Differentiation comes from AI scribe, payments, telehealth and marketplace demand.

**athenahealth (US enterprise)**
- Mostly priced as a percentage of collections (estimates range from ~4–8% up to ~10%), or about $140+ per provider — [CostBench](https://costbench.com/software/ehr-emr/athenahealth/); [Pabau](https://pabau.com/blog/athenahealth-emr-cost/).

### Inferences
- Pricing archetypes to choose from:
  - (a) Flat tiers by practitioner count: Cliniko bands. This is simplest and most predictable.
  - (b) Base fee plus a per-practitioner fee: Jane, SimplePractice.
  - (c) Freemium plus usage: Halaxy, charging for SMS, telehealth and payments.
  - (d) Pay per new patient: Zocdoc.
  - (e) Percentage of collections: athenahealth.
- For a low-income, cash market like Syria, (a) or (c) look most transferable. (d) creates the referral-fee and "pay for cancelled bookings" friction documented below.
- Multi-location and unlimited non-clinical users are given away free by Cliniko and Jane. The competitive norm is to charge for clinicians, not for receptionists or branches.
- Doctoralia's VIP tier sells better search ranking. Mixing marketplace ranking with SaaS tiers is lucrative, but it creates perceived-fairness issues and regulatory risk (see the Doctolib antitrust case and Jameda in Germany).

### Gaps
- No official, current Doctolib price list was found; figures are from aggregators and reviews.
- No official TuoTempo pricing was found. Zocdoc's current per-booking dollar ranges are not published on its page (the 2019 range was $35–110).
- Elation Health and Curve Dental official pricing were not found. Dentally pricing comes from a single aggregator.
- No authoritative source covered white-label/custom-domain booking pages or the public API for most vendors. Only Okadoc explicitly advertises embedding its booking into a provider's own website or app (see section 2). Jane sells a $59 website add-on.

## 2. MENA, Turkey and Syria/Levant players (incl. Arabic/RTL, WhatsApp, cash, local payments)

### Takeaway
- **Funded MENA leaders are marketplace-first and have diversified into adjacent services:**
  - Vezeeta: bookings, pharmacy, labs, home visits and subscription plans.
  - Altibbi: telehealth.
  - Okadoc: subscription booking with white-label embedding.
  - Clinicy: Saudi subscription clinic SaaS.
- **Syria itself has only tiny 2026-vintage entrants:**
  - Moadna: 550+ providers, 3,500 bookings.
  - DoctorBook: 200 doctors.
  - A government MoH booking portal for public hospitals.
- Aleppo/Azaz appears to have no dominant private clinic SaaS.

- In the Syrian market, competition is nascent: Moadna's 3,500 total bookings is tiny. The barrier is execution (Arabic UX, offline tolerance, cash, WhatsApp), not incumbents. North-west Syria (Aleppo/Azaz) does not appear in any competitor's stated footprint.
- Vezeeta's showing the clinic's own price, the inference that patients often pay at the clinic, and its 2022 pivot to SaaS subscriptions and owned clinics suggest that pure per-booking commission was not enough to sustain margins in MENA.
- Okadoc's white-label embedding and Clinicy's subscription model are the closest analogues to a multi-tenant SaaS that gives each clinic its own branded booking page.

### Gaps
- No public per-booking fee for Vezeeta (EGP) was found, and no Clinicy, Okadoc or Moadna price lists.

- No information on Arabic/RTL quality or on payment rails available in Syria (sanctions, card acquiring) was researched here.

## 3. Business-model patterns: SaaS-first vs marketplace-first — what worked, numbers, pain points

### Takeaway
- **The biggest winners monetize the provider with recurring subscriptions and keep patients free:**
  - Doctolib: €422M ARR, about 99% from subscriptions.
  - Docplanner: 300k active doctors, 25M bookings/month.
  - Practo: turned profitable in FY25 after years of losses.
- **Per-booking marketplaces such as Zocdoc can work in an insurance-funded, high-ticket market**, but they create persistent doctor friction (referral-fee ethics, paying for cancelled bookings).
- **Several MENA marketplaces added SaaS subscriptions or patient subscriptions** to stabilise revenue.

### Cited Findings
- Doctolib: €422M ARR in 2025, profitable from autumn 2025 — [Maddyness](https://www.maddyness.com/2026/04/07/doctolib-depasse-les-400-millions-de-revenus-et-fait-entrer-de-nouveaux-investisseurs/). About 99% of 2024 revenue came from practitioner subscriptions — [L'Essentiel de l'Éco](https://lessentieldeleco.fr/4114-doctolib-une-rentabilite-fragile-soutenue-par-largent-public/) (attribution unverified).
- Zocdoc:
  - Profitable since 2023; revenue CAGR ~30% — [Fast Company](https://fastcompany.com/91608291/zocdoc-becoming-infrastructure-for-booking-doctor); [Zocdoc](https://www.zocdoc.com/about/news/a-milestone-for-the-team/).
  - The 2019 per-booking switch drew New York doctor criticism — [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/practices/some-new-york-doctors-unhappy-about-zocdoc-s-new-pricing-model-company-says-it-was), followed by a partial retreat — [Healthcare Dive](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/zocdoc-steps-back-on-pricing-change-amid-physician-pushback/529591/).
  - A Change.org petition argues per-booking fees resemble prohibited referral fees — [Change.org](https://www.change.org/p/block-zocdoc-from-violating-professional-medical-ethics-and-making-health-care-expensive).
  - A BBB reviewer says a quoted $35 booking fee became $65 after sign-up — [BBB](https://prod.bbb.org/us/ny/new-york/profile/doctor-referral/zocdoc-0121-133783/customer-reviews?page=369).
  - Fees can apply even when patients cancel — [Zocdoc](https://thepapergown.zocdoc.com/facts/pay-per-booking-fees-explained/).
- Vezeeta: hybrid of transaction fees + SaaS — [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/companies/vezeeta/). Profitable in 2022 after layoffs; subscriptions for 35k+ doctors; moved into owned or integrated clinics — [The National 2022](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/start-ups/2022/10/11/egyptian-healthcare-platform-vezeeta-raises-new-funding-to-fuel-expansion). The move into clinics was partly to get more predictable revenue — [Launchbase Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/?p=11337).
- Practo: FY25 revenue ₹234 crore with GMV of about ₹3,500 crore — [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/business/2025/08/04/dcm46-biz-practo.html). Losses had narrowed to ₹17 crore earlier — [Business Standard](https://business-standard.com/health/health-tech-platform-practo-narrows-losses-to-rs-17-crore-revenue-up-22-125010901144_1.html).
- Clinicy: pure subscription; product profitable, company break-even targeted for mid-2025 — [The National 2024](https://www.thenationalnews.com/future/technology/2024/05/27/how-clinicy-aims-to-efficiently-connect-medical-service-providers-with-patients).
- Okadoc: subscription for providers, free for patients — [The National 2018](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/technology/generation-start-up-okadoc-aims-to-revolutionise-the-medical-booking-system-1.820739).
- Doctoralia monetizes ranking (VIP gets better profile positioning) and lowers payment commissions on higher tiers — [Pabau ES](https://pabau.com/es/blog/precios-de-doctoralia-pro/).
- Regulatory and reputational risk of dominance:
  - Doctolib was fined €4.665M for exclusivity clauses and tying — [Le Club des Juristes](https://www.leclubdesjuristes.com/en-bref/doctolib-sanctionne-pour-abus-de-position-dominante-12848/).
  - Polish courts generally side with ZnanyLekarz on negative reviews ("doctors must expect harsh ratings") — [mp.pl](https://www.mp.pl/pacjent/aktualnosci/123973,sad-lekarz-musi-liczyc-sie-z-tym-ze-bedzie-oceniany-rowniez-surowo).
  - Germany's Federal Court of Justice (BGH) upheld review platforms' freedom of communication — [Fieldfisher](https://www.fieldfisher.com/en/services/privacy-security-and-information/privacy-security-and-information-law-blog/german-federal-court-further-strengthens-review-platforms).
  - A 2018 German ruling let a doctor have her Jameda profile removed. This is based on the [IPKat headline](https://ipkitten.blogspot.com/2018/02/german-fcj-doctors-can-have-their.html); my recollection is that it was because Jameda showed paid competitors' ads on non-paying doctors' profiles, but the article was not read.

### Inferences
- **For Syria, the SaaS-first approach (clinic pays a flat, low monthly fee; patient app free) is better supported by the evidence than per-booking commission:**
  - Clinic fees are small in absolute terms.
  - Doctors resist paying per patient, and it is ethically fraught.
  - Patients may book, then pay cash at the clinic, so a booking commission is hard to collect and enforce. The Vezeeta review showed a clinic treating an app booking as a walk-in.
- **A patient marketplace is still valuable as a demand-acquisition hook for the SaaS (Doctolib's and Docplanner's playbook).** Sell visibility or premium placement transparently and avoid exclusivity clauses.
- **Patient-side subscriptions are a possible second revenue line once volume exists:** Vezeeta's Shamel family discount plans priced in local currency.

### Gaps
- No audited revenue for Vezeeta, Altibbi, Okadoc or Docplanner group-wide was found; aggregator estimates conflict. There is no published failure post-mortem for MENA booking startups.

## 6. Evidence on no-show rates and the effect of reminders/deposits

### Takeaway
- **Reminders reliably but modestly raise attendance.** Text reminders lift attendance from about 68% to 77% (RR 1.14). SMS and phone calls are about equal, and SMS is cheaper. The randomized evidence for WhatsApp-specific reminders is thin.
- **Saudi outpatient no-show rates range from about 11% to 41% by specialty.**
- **Deposits are supported mostly by modelling and practice surveys, not randomized trials.** Vendor claims of 55–85% reduction are not peer-reviewed.

### Cited Findings
- Cochrane review (Gurol-Urganci et al., 2013; 8 RCTs, 6,615 participants) — [SUPPORT summary of the Cochrane review](https://supportsummaries.epistemonikos.org/support-summaries/show/does-the-use-of-mobile-phone-messaging-reminders-increase-attendance-at-healthcare-appointmentsa):
  - Text reminder vs none: attendance 678 → 773 per 1,000, RR 1.14 (1.03–1.26), moderate certainty.
  - Text vs phone call: RR 0.99, no meaningful difference; cost per attendance may be lower for SMS.
  - Text + postal vs postal: RR 1.10, low certainty.
- Hasvold & Wootton (2011), 29 studies: weighted mean relative change in non-attendance of 34%. Manual phone calls did better than automated reminders (39% vs 29%). Reminder timing (day before vs week before) made no difference — [PMC3188816](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3188816/).
- Meta-analysis of 10 RCTs (8,236 participants): reminders improved attendance, RR 1.11 (1.05–1.19), I² = 83%. Telephone reminders were particularly effective — [JHMHP](https://jhmhp.amegroups.org/article/view/10215/html).
- A 2023 review of behavioural-economic no-show interventions (61 studies) describes existing interventions as having modest effects — [BMC Health Services Research](https://bmchealthservres.biomedcentral.com/counter/pdf/10.1186/s12913-023-10059-9.pdf).
- Saudi no-show rates:
  - About 11.3% across >1M appointments at a hospital (2014 data).
  - About 20% in a Riyadh National Guard AI-prediction study.
  - 34.2% in a Riyadh psychiatric clinic.
  - About 40.7% in Armed Forces hospital ENT clinics.
  - 34.8% no-show or reschedule for outpatient MRI.
  - Sources: [PMC6894458](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6894458); [SAI conference paper](https://ftp.saiconference.com/Downloads/Volume15No8/Paper_12-Prediction_of_Outpatient_No_Show_Appointments.pdf); [Neurosciences Journal](https://nsj.org.sa/content/11/4/293.full.pdf); [Bahrain Medical Bulletin, Dec 2024](https://bahrainmedicalbulletin.com/Dec_2024/BMB-24-821.pdf); [PMC10407751](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10407751).
  - I matched figures to URLs from search snippets; verify each before quoting.
- Deposits:
  - A JORS 2023 model (Zhan & Zhang) found refundable deposits more profitable than overbooking, tested on clinic data. This is a simulation, not a trial — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:taf:tjorxx:v:74:y:2023:i:3:p:811-825).
  - In a veterinary survey, most practices saw fewer no-shows after adopting deposit policies; a few reported negative client reaction — [Today's Veterinary Business](https://todaysveterinarybusiness.com/?p=9746).
  - A Lebanese vendor blog claims a 30% drop at a dermatology clinic. Anecdotal — [dev.to](https://dev.to/khayal_hassanieh_95c776fc/reducing-no-shows-with-deposit-policies-in-baabda-clinics-51da).
- Vendor-reported MENA effects: Clinicy reports a 55% average and up to 85% no-show reduction — [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/future/technology/2024/05/27/how-clinicy-aims-to-efficiently-connect-medical-service-providers-with-patients), and 75% per MEVP — [MEVP](https://www.mevp.com/news/saudi-healthtech-startup-clinicy-secures-major-series-a-funding-and-celebrates-one-million-patients-served).
- Clinicy's CEO estimates Saudi healthcare loses about SAR 3B (~$800M) a year to admin inefficiency and missed appointments — [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/future/technology/2024/05/27/how-clinicy-aims-to-efficiently-connect-medical-service-providers-with-patients).

### Inferences
- **Build automated reminders (SMS and WhatsApp) with confirm/cancel buttons, and expect roughly a 10-percentage-point attendance gain**, not the 50–85% vendors claim.
- Freed slots should flow to a waitlist or the walk-in queue.
- Make deposits optional, per clinic and per service (e.g., dental/cosmetic procedures). The evidence is weak and Syrian payment rails are limited.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed RCT showing a significant WhatsApp-reminder effect was located. A 2024 Punjab RCT (n=388, non-significant) was mentioned only by a secondary source, and the primary paper was not retrieved.
- No no-show data for Syria, Jordan, Lebanon or Turkey private clinics was found.

## 7. Common gaps and complaints in reviews that a new entrant could exploit

### Takeaway
Recurring pain points:
- **Price:** rising subscriptions (Doctoralia, SimplePractice), annual lock-ins, per-booking fees even on cancellations (Zocdoc).
- **Platform power:** exclusivity, tying, paid ranking (Doctolib, Doctoralia).
- **Clinic-app mismatch:** clinics ignoring app bookings (Vezeeta).
- **Support quality.**
- **Weak multi-location for SMB tools:** Practo Ray.

### Cited Findings
- Doctoralia Pro:
  - Capterra summary: "pricing is expensive, with frequent increases," hurting individuals and small practices, despite 96% positive sentiment across 409 reviews and 4.7 ease of use — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/253301/Doctoralia-Pro/reviews/).
  - A reviewer says the price rose "quickly and unfairly" and support is hard to reach — [Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/appointment-scheduling/doctoralia-pro-profile/reviews/).
  - A consumer complaint concerns an annual "Premium" lock-in — [OCU](https://www.ocu.org/reclamar/empresas/doctoralia/c171c484f900cca285).
- Zocdoc: fees on cancelled visits; waivers at Zocdoc's discretion — [Zocdoc](https://thepapergown.zocdoc.com/facts/pay-per-booking-fees-explained/). A quoted fee increased after sign-up — [BBB](https://prod.bbb.org/us/ny/new-york/profile/doctor-referral/zocdoc-0121-133783/customer-reviews?page=369).
- Doctolib: exclusivity clauses and tying sanctioned — [Le Club des Juristes](https://www.leclubdesjuristes.com/en-bref/doctolib-sanctionne-pour-abus-de-position-dominante-12848/). Price changes allowed with 1 month notice — [Hackceleration](https://hackceleration.com/labs/switch/doctolib).
- Vezeeta app reviews — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/eg/app/vezeeta/id1010281314):
  - A same-day appointment was cancelled, and a doctor was absent.
  - A clinic used its own reservation system and treated an app booking as a walk-in, and support did not resolve it.
  - Pharmacy orders were undelivered.
  - The app was "cumbersome" and visit history was missing (Dec 2025).
- Practo Ray: limited multi-location/hospital scalability — [Zoho](https://www.zoho.com/healthcare/resources/guides/best-practice-management-software-2026.html?src=dd); weaker for multi-location and specialty workflows — [FitGap](https://us.fitgap.com/products/ray-by-practo).
- Jane: Balance plan capped at 20 appointments/month; AI scribe, insurance and payroll are paid add-ons — [Jane](https://jane.app/pricing).
- SimplePractice: Starter rose from $29 to $49 in 2025 — [PricingSaaS](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/simplepractice/diffs/2025Q1).
- athenahealth is seen as high-cost for small practices — [CostBench](https://costbench.com/software/ehr-emr/athenahealth/).
- Turkey's public MHRS has weeks-long waits that push patients to private care — [BirGün](https://www.birgun.net/haber/hastanelerdeki-randevu-krizi-tbmm-gundeminde-yurttaslar-ozel-hastanelere-yuksek-ucretler-odemek-zorunda-kaliyor-622924). This is a demand driver for private booking.

### Inferences
Exploitable positioning for a Syrian entrant:
- **Price:** transparent flat pricing in practitioner bands, no lock-in, published price list, no per-booking fees.
- **Booking sync:** a single source of truth, so app bookings and walk-ins live in the same clinic queue. This fixes Vezeeta's "booking ignored" failure mode.
- **Multi-location:** genuine multi-branch support from day one.
- **No platform lock-in:** no exclusivity, fair ranking that is disclosed if paid, and data export.
- **Support:** local Arabic support, e.g. over WhatsApp.

### Gaps
- I did not systematically mine Google Play reviews for Vezeeta, Altibbi or Okadoc, or G2 reviews for Cliniko and Jane, so their complaint themes are not quantified.
- No Syrian user reviews of Moadna or DoctorBook were found.
